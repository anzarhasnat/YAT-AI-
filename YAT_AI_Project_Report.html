ABSTRACT

YAT AI – AI-POWERED MOCK INTERVIEW & CANDIDATE EVALUATION PLATFORM

Technical placement examinations and recruitment interviews are pivotal milestones for diploma and engineering students. However, students frequently face difficulties evaluating their practical readiness because conventional preparation methods rely on static question banks, unassisted self-study, or disjointed coding websites. Students rarely receive real-time, constructive feedback on their technical problem-solving logic, code efficiency, or resume project explanations. This creates a critical need for an intelligent, accessible, and automated platform that provides realistic mock evaluations.

YAT AI – AI-Powered Mock Interview Platform is a web-based application developed to provide students with a comprehensive, centralized environment for practicing, analyzing, and mastering technical recruitment interviews. The system allows authenticated users to upload their PDF resume, which is parsed by AI to extract skills, frameworks, and projects. Based on this profile, YAT AI conducts a dynamic 5-round technical evaluation covering: (1) Core CS Fundamentals & MCQs, (2) Deep-Dive Technical Inquiry, (3) System Design & Architecture, (4) Live Algorithmic Coding Challenge, and (5) Behavioral & HR Evaluation.

Following the interview, the platform generates a comprehensive Candidate DNA Report that visualizes performance across multi-dimensional competency vectors (Logic, Algorithmic Efficiency, System Architecture, Core CS Depth, and Communication). The application also supports Training and Placement Officer (TPO) placement drives and job-specific practice rooms.

The application is developed using modern web technologies, including Next.js 15 (React 19), TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, and Framer Motion with a premium Glassy Emerald UI. Supabase (PostgreSQL) is used for database operations and authentication with Row-Level Security (RLS), while Groq SDK and Google Gemini Flash provide high-throughput AI services with automated failover.

The project demonstrates the development of an integrated assessment platform that combines real-time AI evaluation, resume intelligence, and performance telemetry within a unified web application.

CHAPTER 1

INTRODUCTION

1.1 Project Overview

YAT AI is an intelligent, AI-powered mock interview and technical evaluation platform developed to provide students with a comprehensive, realistic, and accessible environment for practicing and mastering technical job interviews. The project focuses on bridging the gap between theoretical classroom learning and the rigorous standards of campus placements and industry recruitment.

In modern computer science and engineering education, candidates are evaluated across a diverse range of competencies, including core theoretical concepts, real-time algorithmic problem solving, project architecture defense, and behavioral communication. However, students frequently face difficulties assessing their practical interview readiness because conventional preparation relies on static multiple-choice questions or unassisted self-study.

YAT AI addresses this challenge by providing a dedicated technical recruitment platform where students can experience multi-round interview simulations. The application incorporates automated PDF resume parsing, interactive 5-round evaluations, live code analysis, and a multidimensional Candidate DNA Matrix that diagnoses strengths and areas for improvement.

The project is developed using modern web technologies and follows a structured full-stack architecture that integrates Next.js 15, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, Supabase PostgreSQL, and advanced AI services including Groq Cloud SDK and Google Gemini Flash. Through this approach, YAT AI provides students with a unified and accessible environment for accelerating career readiness.

7

1.2 Problem Statement

Students preparing for campus placements and technical evaluations encounter significant friction due to the scattered and static nature of available preparation tools:

  • Fragmented Preparation Environments: Students commonly switch between multiple disjointed websites—solving coding puzzles on one platform, reviewing theoretical computer science notes on another, and practicing communication with peers who lack objective scoring standards.
  • Generic, Non-Personalized Inquiries: Standard practice tests rely on fixed question banks that fail to tailor inquiries to the candidate's actual projects, declared technical skills, or target engineering roles.
  • Absence of Diagnostic Feedback: Traditional quiz applications output a simple total score or percentage, without providing actionable explanations of why an architectural choice was suboptimal or how code efficiency can be improved.
  • Scalability Constraints in Educational Institutions: Conducting mock interviews manually requires significant faculty time and resources. In polytechnic colleges with hundreds of candidates, professors cannot conduct repeated individual 45-minute interviews for every student.

Therefore, there is an urgent need for a centralized, intelligent mock interview platform that automates multi-round evaluations, analyzes candidate code in real time, and provides personalized diagnostic feedback based on individual student resumes.

BACKGROUND

2.1 Background of the Problem

The transition from academic diploma coursework to professional software engineering employment has historically presented a steep challenge for students. While academic curricula provide vital theoretical foundations in programming languages, data structures, and database management, competitive placement drives evaluate real-time problem-solving agility, practical system design, and clear articulation under evaluation constraints.

Traditionally, students prepare for technical assessments using:

  • Static Textbooks and Question Compendiums: Memorizing answers to standard questions without interactive challenge or personalized follow-ups.
  • Algorithmic Practice Websites: Solving standalone puzzles that grade code solely on test cases while completely neglecting verbal defense, architectural trade-offs, and communication.
  • Informal Peer Mocks: Conducting mock interviews with classmates who often lack standardized rubrics, industry experience, or objective benchmarks.

These limitations highlight the necessity of an automated, objective evaluation platform that combines dynamic questioning, resume grounding, and real-time analytical feedback within a single cohesive system.

3.1 Technologies and Tools Used

The development of YAT AI involves modern, production-grade web technologies, frameworks, libraries, and cloud services. The technology stack was chosen to guarantee type safety, high execution speed, maintainable modular code, and cloud scalability.

3.1.1 Frontend Technologies

  • Next.js 15 (React 19): Full-stack framework utilizing React Server Components, server actions, dynamic routing, and fast edge rendering.
  • TypeScript: Introduces static typing across client components and backend APIs, catching type mismatches during development.
  • Tailwind CSS: Utility-first styling framework used to engineer the signature Glassy Emerald design system with frosted-glass backdrops (`backdrop-blur-md`), glowing borders, and dark-mode styling.
  • Framer Motion: Declarative animation library powering smooth page transitions, progress bars, interactive modal dialogs, and SVG radar charts.
18

3.1.2 Backend Technologies

  • Next.js Route Handlers (App Router API): Server-side TypeScript endpoints handling interview step transitions, prompt assembly, and response validation.
  • Groq Cloud SDK: High-throughput inference gateway running ultra-fast open-weight models (qwen/qwen3.8-27b, openai/gpt-oss-120b).
  • Google Gemini Flash API: Multimodal LLM endpoint used for high-context resume text extraction and Candidate DNA synthesis.
  • Resilient Failover Engine: Custom sequential model retry mechanism that seamlessly reroutes API calls during rate limits or model unavailability.

3.1.3 Database and Cloud Storage

  • Supabase (PostgreSQL 15): Cloud relational database storing user profiles, interview sessions, transcripts, job postings, and campus drives.
  • Supabase Auth: Authentication service providing Google OAuth 2.0 and JWT session management.
  • Row-Level Security (RLS): Database-level policies ensuring authenticated candidates can only read and write their own interview records.
  • Supabase Cloud Storage: Secure bucket for storing uploaded candidate PDF resumes.

3.2 System Design / Architecture

System architecture describes the organization of the major software components and explains how they communicate to perform required operations. YAT AI follows a decoupled 4-tier full-stack architecture:

3.2.1 Presentation Layer

The presentation layer delivers the user interface through which candidates interact with the platform. Built using Next.js 15, React 19, and Tailwind CSS, this layer renders the authentication views, student dashboard, resume upload zone, 5-round interactive interview rooms, and Candidate DNA radar visualizers.

3.2.2 Application & Business Logic Layer

The server route handlers process application requests, validate incoming payloads, verify authentication tokens, construct system prompts, and coordinate operations between the AI gateway and the database.

3.2.3 AI Inference & Failover Layer

This layer interfaces with Groq Cloud and Google Gemini Flash APIs. It handles strict JSON schema enforcement, token truncation guards, latency optimization, and automated model failover.

3.2.4 Data Management & Persistence Layer

Supabase PostgreSQL maintains relational tables for candidate profiles, interview transcripts, and placement drives, protected by Row-Level Security policies.

3.5.7 Error Handling and Platform Security

To ensure high stability and user data privacy, the backend incorporates multi-layered defensive programming:

  • JWT Token Verification: All interview and profile modification endpoints verify session tokens against Supabase Auth.
  • Strict JSON Formatting: System prompts enforce structured JSON generation, paired with regex-based markdown extraction guards.
  • Rate Limit Retry (HTTP 429): Implements exponential backoff delays (1500ms, 3000ms, 6000ms) to smoothly navigate API quotas.
  • Input Validation & Sanitization: Truncates large resume payloads to prevent token overflow attacks and request timeout errors.

CHAPTER 5

CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK

5.1 Conclusion

The YAT AI project demonstrates the successful design, implementation, and cloud deployment of a full-stack, AI-driven mock interview and candidate evaluation platform for diploma and engineering students. By uniting modern full-stack web technologies—Next.js 15 (React 19), TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, and Supabase PostgreSQL—with high-throughput generative AI gateways (Groq Cloud SDK and Google Gemini Flash), the platform delivers a realistic and accessible interview practice environment.

The platform achieves all primary project objectives:

  • Parses PDF resumes to ground inquiries in candidate projects.
  • Guides candidates through a realistic 5-round evaluation pipeline.
  • Delivers actionable diagnostic feedback via the Candidate DNA Matrix.
  • Provides placement cells with streamlined campus drive coordination.

YAT AI addresses the acute shortage of personalized technical preparation tools in diploma colleges, empowering students to build self-confidence and excel in competitive recruitment drives.

43

5.2 Limitations of the Project

While YAT AI meets all core requirements, certain technical constraints remain:

  • Text-Centric Evaluation: Candidate answers are submitted through text and code; real-time facial expression and speech tone analysis are not included.
  • Third-Party Cloud API Dependency: Requires an active Internet connection to communicate with Groq and Gemini API endpoints.
  • Language Scope: Question generation and evaluation rubrics operate exclusively in English.
  • Scanned PDF Handling: Non-selectable image-based PDF resumes require OCR preprocessing for optimal text extraction.

5.3 Future Scope / Future Work

Future development phases for YAT AI will explore:

  • Bidirectional Voice AI: Integrating WebRTC audio streaming for realistic voice-to-voice phone screening simulations.
  • Webcam Proctoring: Utilizing computer vision models to evaluate candidate eye contact, posture, and poise.
  • Institutional LMS Integration: Supporting single-sign-on (SSO) and grade synchronization with platforms like Moodle and Canvas.
  • Offline Mobile Application: Building cross-platform mobile apps (React Native) with offline question caching for practice in low-bandwidth areas.
